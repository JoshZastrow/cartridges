Cartridge drift

What this shows

  • Drift is how far training moved the cartridge away from its starting point. The starting point is the cache the base model produces when it reads the first tokens of the real text (the teacher's context block).
  • Relative drift = ‖trained − init‖ / ‖init‖. 0 means unchanged; 1 means the change is as large as the original cache.
  • Cosine is the cosine similarity between init and trained (1 = same direction). How it is computed: in the overview, for attention layers it is computed per position (each position's heads × head_dim vector) and then averaged over positions; for DeltaNet states it is the cosine of the whole flattened tensor. Section 1b uses the cosine of the whole flattened tensor for every layer type, because only then do drift, norm change and angle fit together exactly.
  • Attention layers store keys and values: one vector per position (heads × head_dim). Position 0, the attention sink, is frozen and left out. DeltaNet layers store a recurrent state and a conv state instead of per-position vectors.
  • The page holds only numbers and position indices. No journal text or tokens are embedded.

1. Overview by layer

Relative drift of each layer's cache. Circles are attention layers (keys, values); squares are DeltaNet layers (recurrent state, conv state). The lower panel shows the mean cosine similarity.

1b. Rotation or growth?

Each layer's change, measured on the whole flattened tensor, split three ways: norm change ‖trained‖/‖init‖ − 1; angle arccos(cosine) between init and trained; and the perpendicular share ‖Δ⊥‖/‖Δ‖, the part of the change Δ at right angles to init.

Measured cosine against drift, with two reference models. Perpendicular push: Δ is perpendicular to init, so the vector turns and also gets longer by √(1+drift²); this predicts cos = 1/√(1+drift²). Pure rotation: length kept, direction turned; this predicts cos = 1 − drift²/2.

2. Layer explorer (PCA)

Each position of the chosen layer gives one vector (heads × head_dim), before and after training. Every th position is shown; color = position. Three projections:

  • Joint PCA (default): PCA fitted on init and trained vectors together, so the 2D plane includes the init→trained shift. Open circles are init, filled dots are trained, and a thin line joins each position's pair.
  • Displacement PCA: PCA of the moves themselves, Δ = trained − init, without centering, so a shift that all positions share shows up as direction 1. The bar chart gives each direction's share of the total movement. A few tall bars mean positions moved in a few shared directions; a flat spectrum means the movement is diffuse.
  • Init-only PCA: PCA fitted on the init vectors only. Caveat: almost all of the movement points out of this plane (see the in-plane share), so short lines here do not mean small drift.

If the in-plane share is small even for joint PCA, the movement has no dominant shared direction: it is spread over many dimensions. The displacement spectrum shows how spread out it is.
