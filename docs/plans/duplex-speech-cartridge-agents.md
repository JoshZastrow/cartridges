# Multi-agent instructions: implementing the duplex speech cartridge

Companion to `docs/plans/duplex-speech-cartridge.md` (the plan: survey,
requirements, spec). Tracking: epic #7, issues #8–#18 on the Cartridges
board. This file says how several Claude Code agents build it without
tripping over each other. The rules in `CLAUDE.md` (claim an issue before
working, worktree per issue, never commit to `main`, Status on the board is
the truth) apply unchanged; this file only adds what is specific to this epic.

## 1. What we are building, in one paragraph

A trained KV prefix ("cartridge") for NVIDIA PersonaPlex 7B that replaces its
voice-prompt + text-prompt with learned keys and values per temporal layer,
so that the model **talks as the author**: in the author's voice and
prosody, in the author's diction, in the first person, and with content
lifted more or less verbatim from the author's journal. This is not the text
cartridge's job (answer facts and insights *about* the author). It is a
conversational self-portrait: asked "how have you been feeling lately?", the
model tells the story from the most recent journal entry in the author's own
sentences; asked "how have you changed over the years?", it answers "in 2024
I believed <what the author wrote then>, but lately I've been focusing on
<what the author wrote recently>". The cartridge is initialised from a
prefill of the author's voice clip, a persona text and a journal digest, then
trained by self-study on synthetic conversations whose answers are composed
from the author's own journal sentences and rendered in the author's voice
(stage 1), then refined on its own rollouts (stage 2). The base model stays
frozen. Serving needs a pinned prefix region in the ring KV cache so the
cartridge is never evicted.

The same cartridge recipe is trained **in parallel on hertz-dev**, the
textless audio-only model closest to the Dessa profile, through one backend
interface (section 4b). Where PersonaPlex reads persona and digest as text,
hertz-dev hears them spoken in the author's voice; where PersonaPlex is
distilled on its text stream, hertz-dev is distilled on its discrete
audio-token stream. Both are evaluated with the same protocol (section 8)
and reported side by side.

## 1b. Target behaviour and dataset design

**Target behaviour.** With the cartridge and no other prompt, PersonaPlex:

1. speaks in the author's voice (acoustic identity and prosody);
2. speaks in the author's diction: the author's words, sentence shapes,
   recurring phrases and rhythm, in the first person, spoken register;
3. answers conversational prompts with the author's own journal content,
   retold rather than summarised: stories, ideas, moods, as written;
4. respects time: "lately", "these days" map to the most recent entries;
   "over the years", "back then" produce chronologically ordered contrasts
   between entries at least a year apart;
5. never produces content about sexuality or anger, and declines gently
   in character if asked;
6. keeps PersonaPlex's duplex behaviour (backchannels, interruptions).

**Unit of data: the passage.** A passage is one to several consecutive
sentences from one journal entry, with its date and page. Passages are the
only source of response content. The Qwen Teacher (full journal) selects
them; it does not paraphrase them.

**Topic exclusion, first.** Before any question is written, the Qwen Teacher
labels every passage with one of `{sexuality, anger, other}` (plus a
`borderline` flag). `sexuality`, `anger` and `borderline` passages are
removed from the pool and from the digest. A second classifier pass runs on
every generated response and every teacher rollout transcript; any hit is
dropped and counted. The counts go in the #10 summary comment. The held-out
eval includes 20 prompts that invite those topics, to measure the
in-character decline (target: 0 violations).

**Questions.** Conversational, the kind a friend asks, generated by the Qwen
Teacher *from* the selected passages so that each has a grounded answer.
Three families, roughly 50 / 30 / 20:

- *Story* (one passage): "how have you been feeling lately?", "what's been
  on your mind?", "tell me about <place/person/project named in the
  passage>", "what happened with <event>?".
- *Change* (two or more passages ≥ 1 year apart): "how have you changed
  over the years?", "do you still think that about <topic>?", "what did
  you believe back then?".
- *Idea* (one passage with an opinion or reflection): "what do you make of
  <theme>?", "why does <thing> matter to you?".

The author's own questions and conversational chat prompts from the task DB
are kept as a fourth, small family; psychology-test items are dropped (not
conversational).

**Responses.** Composed by the Qwen Teacher under hard constraints, then
validated mechanically:

- ≥ 80% of response tokens appear verbatim in the source passages (measured
  as n-gram coverage, n = 4, against the passage text); the rest are bridging
  words only: dates, "back then", "lately", "and", "but".
- First person throughout; no third-person references to "the author" or
  "the journal"; no spoken citations (`[Journal:p.N]` never appears in the
  spoken text; page refs live in metadata for validation).
- Spoken register: 2–6 sentences, 10–40 s when rendered; contractions
  allowed; no lists, no headings.
- Chronology stated in words when two passages are used ("in 2024 … but
  this spring …"), in the order written.
- Passes the topic classifier.

Rejected responses are regenerated once, then the passage is skipped. Target
pool: ~3,000 train, ~200 held-out, with held-out passages disjoint from train
passages and spanning every journal year.

**Audio.** The response text is rendered in the author's voice by **Kyutai
Pocket TTS** (100M, clones a voice from one short sample, batch inference,
runs on CPU or Apple Silicon), conditioned on the author's prompt split
from `data/voice/`; aligned to words with Phonon-2 word timestamps; resampled to
24 kHz and Mimi-encoded. This is the agent audio target. The question is
rendered in other voices on the user channel (Pocket TTS with other donated
voices, or any open TTS). This replaces "teacher rollouts in PersonaPlex's
prompt voice" as the primary target: the voice we are teaching is the
author's, and the words are the author's. Pocket TTS's own similarity to
the author's held-out clips is measured first and is the ceiling; if it is
below 0.7, fine-tune Pocket TTS on the author's clips before rendering
(a sub-task of #10) rather than accepting a low ceiling.

**Objective.** Stage 1 is PersonaPlex's own SFT loss on the agent streams
(text tokens placed at their word frames with pads between, Mimi tokens of
the author's-voice audio; semantic codebook 1, acoustic 0.02, padded text
0.3), applied to the frozen model with only the cartridge trainable. A KL
term on the *text stream only* to a PersonaPlex teacher that has the source
passage in its `<system>` prompt is kept as a regulariser for fluency and
turn-taking (weight to sweep in #14; start at 0.5). Stage 2 keeps the
on-policy loop with that same teacher on the text stream and the author's
TTS as the audio reference for sampled text.

**What "good" means.** Three measures, all against held-out prompts:

- *Voice*: speaker similarity to the author's held-out clips (≥ stock
  prompt baseline, then ≥ 0.7 with the LoRA phase).
- *Diction and content*: verbatim 4-gram coverage of the transcript against
  the journal (target ≥ 0.6 on story prompts, where the ceiling set by the
  responses is ~0.8); first-person rate ≥ 0.95; a Qwen judge rubric
  `voice-v0` scoring "is this how the author writes" and "is the retold
  content actually in the journal", with the matching passages shown to the
  judge.
- *Behaviour*: duplex metrics within 10% of baseline; 0 topic violations.

## 2. Roles

- **Coordinator (one agent, no worktree).** Owns the board. Re-checks blockers
  when an issue moves to Done, flips the next issues to Ready, reads every
  summary comment, and keeps section 5 of this file (interface contracts)
  current. Does not write feature code.
- **Implementer (one agent per issue, own worktree).** Claims one Ready issue,
  builds exactly its acceptance criteria, opens a PR with `Closes #N`, posts
  the summary comment. Never edits files listed under another In Progress
  issue without commenting there first.
- **Reviewer (optional, one agent per PR).** Reviews against the issue's
  acceptance list and the contracts in section 5, nothing else.

Run at most four implementers at once (the four Ready issues of wave 1).
More than that only creates merge conflicts in `voice/src/voice/`.

## 3. Waves (dependency order)

**Strategy: evaluation-driven, inference first.** The evals are as much
the product as the model. The first thing that must exist is a loop that
scores a *deployed* model against a versioned eval set and records the
result in W&B; every later issue adds a point to that record. So the order
is: eval set and stock inference endpoints, then the harness and the two
baselines (the wave-1 exit criterion), then cartridge mechanics, then
training, then redeploy the variant and re-run the same eval. Training
itself runs the smoke eval at every validation step, so a checkpoint is
promoted on eval numbers, not on loss.

| Wave | Issues | Can run in parallel | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | #19 bootstrap `voice/` and `shared/`, backend protocol, registry, generic trainer skeleton | alone | root tests unchanged; `voice train --dry-run` on a fake backend |
| 1a | #20 PersonaPlex stock inference + serve + deploy + client · #21 hertz-dev stock inference + serve + deploy · #22 eval set artifact (smoke + full) | all three | two stock endpoints answer the client; the first `voice-evalset` version in W&B (`v0`) |
| 1b | #9 eval harness and report against the endpoints (needs #20, #21, #22) | alone | **two baseline rows in W&B**, produced by `voice eval --size full` against `voice-serve`, plus the smoke eval under 10 min per system |
| 2 | #8 PersonaPlex cartridge half + parity (needs #20) · #10 training data (needs #22) · #11 teacher study (needs #9, #10) | all three | step-0 cartridge equals the stock prompt; train dialogues on the volume |
| 3 | #18 hertz-dev cartridge half + parity (needs #21, #8: one wave behind, so #8's lessons carry over) · #12 pinned cache + `voice deploy --system <cartridge>` (needs #8) · #13 teacher targets (needs #11, #10) | all three | a cartridge can be served and the full eval re-run against it |
| 4 | #14 stage 1 (needs #12, #13; hertz run when #18 is Done), ending with deploy of the best checkpoint and a full eval | alone | cartridge rows next to baseline rows in W&B, frontier plot updated |

From wave 3 on, every issue runs for both backends behind `--backend`; an
issue is Done when both backends pass its acceptance list, or when the
summary comment says which backend failed and why. Every issue from wave 4
on ends the same way: deploy the result, run the full eval against the
deployment, log it in the experiment group, update the report.

The coordinator flips an issue to Ready only when every "Blocked by" issue is
Done (merged). Do not start a Blocked issue "to get ahead": its contracts are

## 4. Shared conventions for this epic

- **The voice project is its own package, isolated from the cartridge
  work.** It lives in `voice/` at the repo root, as a uv workspace member
  named `voice`, with its own `pyproject.toml`, `src/voice/`, `tests/`,
  README, Modal apps, volumes, W&B project and CLI. Nothing under
  `src/cartridges/` changes for this epic, and nothing in `voice/` imports
  `cartridges.*`. The text cartridge's training, evals, gateway and vLLM
  serving stay exactly as they are.

  ```
  voice/
    pyproject.toml          # member "voice"; depends on "shared", torch, modal, wandb,
                            # the pinned PersonaPlex moshi fork, pocket-tts, fermion-research (Phonon-2)
    README.md
    src/voice/
      model.py              # load PersonaPlex + Mimi + SentencePiece, freeze
      cache.py              # VoiceCartridge (K/V prefix), training-forward patch
      patches/              # pinned-prefix RingKVCache, applied at import
      prompt.py             # voice clip + persona + digest -> init frames
      passages.py           # journal -> dated passages, topic labels
      data.py               # questions, responses, validators, dialogue/frames IO
      tts.py                # render_author (Pocket TTS), render_other
      teacher.py            # passage-prompted PersonaPlex teacher (text-stream targets)
      train.py              # stage 1 SFT+KL, stage 2 on-policy; Modal app "voice-train"
      evals.py              # Phonon-2 transcripts, speaker sim, coverage, judge glue, duplex metrics
      serve.py              # --cartridge flag for moshi.server / moshi.offline
      deploy.py             # Modal app "voice-serve": the PersonaPlex server with a cartridge
      backends/             # base.py, registry.py, personaplex.py (#8), hertz.py (#18); see 4b
    tests/
  shared/
    pyproject.toml          # member "shared": components both projects need
    src/shared/
      teacher.py            # Qwen Teacher client (vLLM on Modal), context block, render
      tracking.py           # W&B runs and artifacts
      judge.py              # rubric judge over a generate() callable; rubric loader
      targets.py            # top-k target shard read/write (with a stream column)
      optim.py              # relative-LR Adam with warmup, EarlyStopping
    tests/
  src/cartridges/           # untouched
  ```

- **Two backends, one pipeline.** PersonaPlex and hertz-dev are trained in
  parallel behind one interface (section 4b). Everything upstream of
  encoding (passages, questions, responses, TTS renders) is shared and
  computed once; everything from encoding on is a backend method; the
  trainer, the evals and the report are generic and take `--backend`.

- **The shared library is copied out, not moved.** `shared/` starts as
  adapted copies of `cartridges.cartridge.{teacher,tracking,tasks.judge,
  tasks.targets}` and the optimizer and `EarlyStopping` from
  `train_distill.py`, with the journal-specific assumptions (citation
  reminder, page-tree paths, Qwen-only prompts) made explicit parameters.
  The cartridge package keeps its own copies until a separate issue, after
  the open cartridge PRs land, switches it to import from `shared`. Until
  then the two copies may drift; that is accepted so this epic never edits
  cartridge files. Anything voice needs from the cartridge repo beyond those
  five modules is copied into `shared/` the same way, with a comment naming
  its origin.
- **Deployment and serving are separate too.** The voice server is
  PersonaPlex's own WebSocket server with the cartridge loaded (`deploy.py`,
  Modal app `voice-serve`), not the LibreChat gateway or vLLM. Volumes are
  `voice-data` (passages, dialogues, frames) and `voice-voice` (the author's
  clips); W&B project `voice`; CLI entry point `voice` (`uv run voice ...`).
- **PersonaPlex is vendored and pinned.** The `moshi` fork from
  `NVIDIA/personaplex` at commit `3428dfd`, a git dependency in
  `voice/pyproject.toml` and installed into the Modal image from
  `voice/src/voice/train.py` (#8 sets this up; everyone else reuses the
  image). Patches to the fork (#12) live in `voice/src/voice/patches/` and
  are applied at import, not committed into the vendored tree.
  The fork's own pins (torch<2.5, numpy<2.2, huggingface-hub<0.25, …)
  conflict with the root lock, so the root `pyproject.toml` carries
  `[tool.uv] override-dependencies` for them (coordinator decision on #19)
  and the fork runs on the root versions (torch 2.14, numpy 2.x, hf-hub
  1.x), which NVIDIA did not test. The Modal image installs the fork with
  `--no-deps` over those versions (`voice.train.image`; reuse it).
- **GPU work runs on Modal, `gpu="H200"`.** Every GPU entry point has a
  `--dry-run` that runs on CPU with a tiny fixture before spending money.
  Spend cap per issue: $40 unless the issue says otherwise; post the W&B
  run and cost in the summary comment. Phase 1 total is estimated at
  $160–200 and exceeds the cap recorded in `docs/plans/next-experiments.md`,
  so the coordinator confirms budget with the author before wave 3.
- **The author's voice.** `data/voice/josh-thoughts-1.m4a` (about 9.8 min,
  git-ignored). #10 converts it to 24 kHz mono WAV at −24 LUFS, splits it
  80/10/10 (TTS prompt and cartridge init / Pocket TTS fine-tune if needed /
  speaker-similarity eval), and uploads it to the Modal Volume
  `voice-voice` with a manifest. Nothing under `data/` is ever
  committed, uploaded to W&B as a public artifact, or pasted into an issue.
  Treat it as personal data.
- **Transcription is Phonon-2.** Every transcript and every word timestamp
  in this epic comes from `FermionResearch/Phonon-2` (English ASR,
  Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 lineage, CC-BY-4.0) through one wrapper
  `evals.py: transcribe(wav) -> {text, words[{w, start_s, end_s, conf}]}`,
  using the MLX build locally and the CUDA Docker image on Modal. `conf` is
  the per-word confidence that the Parakeet-TDT decoder computes natively
  (NeMo's confidence estimation on the TDT/RNNT outputs); #9 confirms
  whether the packaged Phonon-2 CLI exposes it in `--json` and, if it does
  not, obtains it by running the same checkpoint lineage through NeMo's
  decoding with confidence enabled, keeping the transcript text and
  timestamps from Phonon-2. No other recogniser is used anywhere, so
  coverage, WER, alignment and judge inputs are comparable across issues
  and backends.
- **Author-voice TTS is Kyutai Pocket TTS.** All agent-side renders (training
  targets in #10, sampled-text audio references in #15, the ablation in #16)
  use it, from one wrapper `tts.py: render_author(text) -> wav24k`, so every
  issue teaches the same voice. The wrapper also exposes
  `render_other(text, voice_id)` for user-channel questions.
- **Tests.** Every module has a CPU test with synthetic tensors or audio
  under `voice/tests/` (and `shared/tests/` for the library), run with
  `uv run --package voice pytest`. The parity fixture from #8 (stored logits
  of the stock prompt path) is the one GPU-derived fixture and is committed
  once, small (10 dialogues, 50 frames each, top-20 only).
- **Evals score the deployment.** `voice eval` drives a served endpoint
  through the offline client (#20), audio in and audio out, never a Python
  model object, so what is scored is what ships. `backend.rollout` is used
  only by the in-training smoke eval. Two sizes from the same artifact:
  `smoke` (10 prompts, under 10 minutes, gates every PR and every
  validation step) and `full` (section 8).
- **W&B is the lab notebook.** Project `voice`. Conventions every issue
  follows:
  - *Groups*: one per experiment, `exp-YYYYMMDD-<slug>`, holding the train
    run, its eval runs and a `baseline_run` field pointing at the stock
    baseline it is compared to.
  - *Run names*: `<backend>/<system>/<stage>` where system is `stock` or a
    cartridge artifact name, stage is `eval-smoke`, `eval-full`, `sft`,
    `onpolicy`, `ablation`, `lora`, or `serve-smoke` (a deployment check
    on non-evalset prompts, never reported); tags `baseline` or
    `candidate` for evalset runs only, `check` for `serve-smoke`, and the
    backend name.
  - *Every eval run logs*: config (backend, checkpoint or artifact and
    version, `voice-evalset` version, seeds, judge model and rubric hash,
    Phonon-2 version, git commit); one W&B Table per axis with
    `metric, value, ci_low, ci_high, n`; the summary metrics under fixed
    names (`fidelity/coverage`, `fidelity/judge`, `interaction/fdb15`,
    `responsiveness/latency_s`, `voice/speaker_sim`, `naturalness/pref`);
    the five side-by-sides as `wandb.Audio` with transcripts in a Table;
    GPU-hours and dollars.
  - *Every training run logs*: loss per stream and total, validation loss,
    the smoke-eval summary metrics at every validation step under the same
    names as the eval runs, the checkpoint artifact (`voice-cartridge`,
    aliases `best` and `step-N`), and at the end the id of the full-eval
    run of the promoted checkpoint.
  - *Artifacts*: `voice-evalset` (versioned, #22), `voice-dialogues`
    (training data, #10), `voice-targets` (#13), `voice-cartridge`,
    `voice-report`. Audio under `data/` is never uploaded; the author's
    eval split lives in the artifact under restricted project access.
  - *Report*: `voice report` reads runs from W&B by group and tag, never
    from local files, and writes its outputs back as a `voice-report`
    artifact.
- **Style.** Match `cartridges/cartridge/cache.py`: pure functions where
  possible, IO injected, Pydantic configs, docstring at the top of each
  module stating the shapes. Same ruff settings as the root.

## 4b. Backend interface and factory

One `DuplexBackend` protocol, two implementations, a registry. The data
pipeline, trainer, evals and report never import a backend module directly;
they call `backends.get(name, config)`.

```
voice/src/voice/backends/
  base.py          # BackendSpec, Frames, Cartridge, Outputs, DuplexBackend (Protocol)
  registry.py      # register(name) decorator; get(name, config) -> DuplexBackend
  personaplex.py   # Moshi-style: 17 discrete streams, text + Mimi, 12.5 Hz
  hertz.py         # hertz-dev: 2 channels, FSQ tokens + continuous latents, 8 Hz
```

**Shared types (`base.py`).**

```python
class BackendSpec(BaseModel):
    name: str
    sample_rate: int            # 24000 (PersonaPlex) / 16000 (hertz)
    frame_rate_hz: float        # 12.5 / 8.0
    context_frames: int         # 3000 / 2048
    streams: list[StreamSpec]   # name, role (user|agent), kind (text|audio_token|latent), vocab or dim
    has_text: bool              # True / False
    cache_groups: list[str]     # ["temporal"] / ["lm", "resyn"]

class Frames(BaseModel):        # one dialogue, encoded for one backend
    tokens: dict[str, Tensor]   # stream name -> [T] int (discrete streams)
    latents: dict[str, Tensor]  # stream name -> [T, D] float (continuous streams; hertz only)
    meta: dict                  # dialogue id, words with frame indices, split

class Cartridge(nn.Module):     # trainable prefix; generic container
    groups: dict[str, KVGroup]  # KVGroup: keys[list of L tensors], values[...], num_frozen
    p: int
    meta: dict                  # backend, spec hash, fork commit, voice_sha, digest_sha, stage, step
    def save(dir) / load(dir)   # cartridge.safetensors + cartridge.json, group-prefixed tensor names

class Outputs(BaseModel):
    logits: dict[str, Tensor]   # agent discrete stream -> [B, T, V]
    gmm: dict[str, GMMParams]   # agent latent stream -> (locs, scales, weights); hertz only
```

**The protocol.**

```python
class DuplexBackend(Protocol):
    spec: BackendSpec
    def load(self, device: str) -> None                                   # weights, codec, tokenizer; frozen
    # encoding (after the shared TTS stage)
    def encode_dialogue(self, d: Dialogue) -> Frames                      # user_wav, agent_wav, words, response_text -> Frames
    def decode_agent(self, frames: Frames) -> np.ndarray                  # agent streams -> wav at spec.sample_rate
    # prefix and cartridge
    def build_prefix(self, voice_wav, persona_text, digest_text) -> Frames    # PersonaPlex: hybrid prompt frames;
                                                                              # hertz: Pocket TTS speech of persona+digest on the agent channel
    def cartridge_from_prefill(self, prefix: Frames) -> Cartridge          # run the prefix, keep every cache group
    # training
    def forward(self, frames: Frames, cartridge: Cartridge | None) -> Outputs   # full-sequence, differentiable w.r.t. the cartridge
    def sft_loss(self, out: Outputs, frames: Frames, w: LossWeights) -> dict[str, Tensor]   # backend's own next-frame loss on agent streams
    def distill_loss(self, out: Outputs, targets: Targets) -> Tensor      # KL to stored top-k on the discrete agent streams named in targets
    # generation and serving
    def rollout(self, cartridge, user_wav, max_s: float, temps) -> Dialogue   # streaming decode from the pinned prefix
    def serve(self, cartridge, port) -> Server                            # the backend's live server with the prefix pinned
```

**What each backend does behind the same method.**

| Method | PersonaPlex | hertz-dev |
|---|---|---|
| `encode_dialogue` | Mimi (8 codebooks) for both channels at 24 kHz; agent text tokens at word start frames with pad between; 17 token streams | hertz-codec at 16 kHz → 32-d latents per channel, 8 Hz; FSQ quantizer → 15-bit token per channel; `tokens = {user, agent}`, `latents = {user, agent}`; no text |
| `build_prefix` | voice clip on agent audio + sine on user + `<system>` persona and digest forced one token per frame | Pocket TTS reads persona + digest in the author's voice; voice clip first; all on the agent channel, user channel silent. Budget ≤ 90 s total (720 frames), because context is 256 s |
| `cartridge_from_prefill` | `temporal` group: 32 layers × K/V `[1,32,p,128]` | `lm` group: 32 layers × K/V `[1,32,p,128]`; `resyn` group: 8 + 4 split layers of the resynthesizer, cloned from its caches |
| `forward` | patched `StreamingMultiheadAttention`, RoPE offset `p`, mask extended by `p` | strip the `@torch.no_grad` on `HertzDevModel.forward` / `TransformerVAE.forward`; prepend cache groups; **pass an explicit causal mask** because `GQA._attend` sets `is_causal=False` whenever `q_len != k_len`, which would let the dialogue attend bidirectionally on top of a prefix |
| `sft_loss` | CE on agent text (pad 0.3), semantic codebook 1, acoustic codebooks 0.02 | CE on the agent channel's FSQ index of frame t+1 (`output2` head, roll by one as the resynthesizer does) + GMM NLL of the agent latent at t+1 (`GaussianMixtureIOLayer.loss`); user channel weight 0 |
| `distill_loss` | KL on the text stream to stored top-20 | KL on the agent FSQ stream (vocab 32,768) to stored top-20 |
| `rollout` | `LMGen` with the pinned ring cache | `completion`-style loop from cloned prefix caches, cache length 1024 + p; stop at `context_frames` |
| `serve` | `moshi.server --cartridge` | `inference_server.py` with the prefix caches loaded instead of the prompt wav |

**Factory and config.**

```python
class BackendConfig(BaseModel):            # discriminated union on `name`
    name: Literal["personaplex", "hertz"]
    checkpoint: str                        # HF id or volume path
    p: int = 1024                          # prefix frames (PersonaPlex) / 512 (hertz, = 64 s)
    device: str = "cuda"

backend = backends.get(cfg.name, cfg)      # registry lookup, raises on unknown name
```

`voice encode|train|eval|deploy --backend personaplex|hertz` all go through
this call and nothing else. A third backend (Moshi itself, or a future
textless release) is a new file plus a `@register("name")`.

**Generic trainer (`train.py`).** Backend-agnostic: build or load the
`Cartridge`, optimizer over its parameters only (`shared.optim`), loop over
`Frames` shards, `loss = backend.sft_loss(...) + λ · backend.distill_loss(...)`,
gradient checkpointing requested through `backend.forward`, validation every
N steps (held-out loss, 20 rollouts through the shared evals), early
stopping, W&B artifact. Stage 2 is the same loop with `backend.rollout` in
the batch and the passage-prompted teacher of that backend re-scoring.

**Generic data pipeline (`data.py`, `passages.py`, `tts.py`).** Produces
`Dialogue` objects (wavs + words + text) once; `voice encode --backend X`
turns them into `Frames` shards per backend on the volume. Passage
selection, topic exclusion and response validation are therefore identical
for both backends, by construction.

**hertz-dev specifics that are not in the table.** Checkpoints: duplex LM
`inference_scion_54000.pt` (13.5 GB) and duplex resynthesizer
`inference_care_50000.pt`, plus codec `inference_apatosaurus_95000.pt` and
quantizer `inference_volcano_3.pt`; load with `is_split=True`. Channel 0 is
the human, channel 1 the model. `torch==2.5.1`, CUDA only; the `autocast`
contexts are hard-coded to `cuda`, so the hertz backend asserts a CUDA
device. The codec's streaming path is broken upstream; encode whole wavs
offline. Latents from `latent_from_data` are *sampled* by default; encode
with `get_parameters=True` and use `mu` so targets are deterministic. The
LM is "very prompt-sensitive" by the authors' own note, which is exactly
what a trained prefix exploits.

## 8. Evaluation protocol (after Thinking Machines' "Interaction Models")

Thinking Machines evaluate their interaction model on three axes and plot
them against each other: **responsiveness** (Full-Duplex-Bench v1
turn-taking latency), **interaction quality** (Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5: user
interruption, user backchannel, talking to others, background speech, with
the model given prerecorded audio and required to respond at set times),
and **intelligence** (audio reasoning benchmarks), plus two timed-content
tests judged by an LLM, TimeSpeak and CueSpeak, where a response counts only
if it "conveys the expected meaning and is delivered at the appropriate
time", macro-averaged over scenarios, and side-by-side qualitative examples.
Their post reports no human naturalness study, no speaker-consistency
metric and no statistics; we add those where our goal needs them. The
protocol below is run identically on every backend (PersonaPlex, hertz-dev)
and on every checkpoint, always next to that backend's stock baseline, and
it is what `voice eval` produces.

**Axis 1: responsiveness.**
- Full-Duplex-Bench v1 turn-taking latency (s) and interruption latency (s)
  on the public FDB-v1 audio. Lower is better. Baseline: PersonaPlex 0.17 s
  / 0.24 s from its model card.
- Spurious onset rate: 20 runs × 5 min of user silence; fraction of runs in
  which the agent starts speaking. Baseline PersonaPlex 27.5%.

**Axis 2: interaction quality.**
- Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 scenario suite, four scenarios, averaged, on the
  public audio: user interruption (does the agent stop and yield), user
  backchannel (does it keep going through "yeah", "mm-hm"), talking to
  others (does it stay quiet when the user addresses a third party),
  background speech (does it ignore it). Score as FDB-v1.5 defines it.
- Our own **CueSpeak-style timed test** on the journal conversations: 60
  held-out prompts delivered at scripted times inside a 2-minute user
  audio track that also contains a backchannel, one interruption and 20 s
  of silence. A Qwen judge sees the transcript with timestamps and the
  expected content (the source passages) and passes a response only if the
  meaning is conveyed *and* it starts within 1.0 s of the cue and does not
  talk over the interruption. Report macro-accuracy over the three cue
  types (after a question, after a backchannel, after an interruption).

**Axis 3: fidelity to the author (our "intelligence" axis).**
- Verbatim 4-gram coverage of the transcript against the journal (story
  prompts; target ≥ 0.6, ceiling ≈ 0.8 set by the responses).
- First-person rate ≥ 0.95; zero spoken citations.
- `voice-v0` judge: "written by the author" and "content is in the
  journal", with the matching passages shown; two orders, averaged.
- Chronology: on *change* prompts, the two retold passages appear in
  written order (judge-checked).
- Topic violations on the 20 baited prompts: must be 0.

**Axis 4: voice (not in the TM post; our goal needs it).**
- Speaker similarity (WavLM-TDNN cosine) to the author's held-out clips,
  100 utterances, 95% bootstrap CI.
- Speaker drift: similarity at minute 1 vs minute 4 of a continuous
  conversation (catches ring-cache eviction and prefix decay).
- Intelligibility: on PersonaPlex, agent WER between the Phonon-2 transcript
  and the agent text stream; on hertz-dev, which has no text stream, the
  mean per-word confidence of the transcript (Phonon-2 is Parakeet-TDT
  lineage, and NeMo's Parakeet decoding emits native per-token and per-word
  confidence; see the transcription bullet in section 4), plus the share of
  transcript tokens that are dictionary words. UTMOS on both, so voice
  gains are not bought with intelligibility.

**Axis 5: naturalness (human, small, paired).**
- 30 held-out prompts, two-system A/B pairs (stock vs cartridge, and
  PersonaPlex-cartridge vs hertz-cartridge), 3 raters each, forced choice
  on "sounds more like a real person talking", plus a 1–5 "sounds like the
  author" from the author alone. Report preference rate with a binomial
  CI. This is the only human measure and it is cheap; it answers the one
  question the automatic metrics cannot.

**Reporting.** `voice eval` writes `report.json` with every row as
`{value, ci_low, ci_high, n}`, and `voice report` renders:

1. the table: baseline vs cartridge per backend, all axes;
2. two frontier plots as in the post: axis 3 (fidelity) against axis 2
   (interaction quality) and against axis 1 (latency), one point per
   backend × checkpoint, baselines marked;
3. five side-by-side transcripts (same prompt, both systems, with
   timestamps and the source passage), chosen by the coordinator, not by
   the implementer who trained the checkpoint.

**Rules.** Held-out passages never appear in training. The same seeds, the
same user-channel audio and the same judge prompt are used for every
system in a comparison. A checkpoint is reported only with its baseline row.
No metric is added to the table without a baseline number for it.

   Review before idling" if the teammate's issue is still In Progress.
4. Optional reusable roles in `.claude/agents/`: `voice-implementer.md`
   (tools: Read, Write, Edit, Bash, Grep, Glob; body = section 7 protocol)
   and `voice-reviewer.md` (tools: Read, Grep, Glob, Bash; body = "review
   only against the issue's acceptance list and section 5 contracts").
   Spawn teammates by those types so every teammate gets the same rules.

### 9.2 How the team maps onto the board

- **One task per issue** in the shared task list, named `#N <title>`, with
  the issue's "Blocked by" mirrored as task dependencies, so blocked tasks
  show as blocked and nobody self-claims them early.
- **One teammate per In Progress issue**, named `impl-N` (`impl-19`,
  `impl-20`, …), so later prompts can address it. A teammate owns exactly
  the files its issue lists; two teammates never share a file.
- **Each teammate works in its own git worktree** on `issue-N-<slug>`.
  In-process teammates share the lead's working directory, so the first
  thing a teammate does is `git worktree add ../cartridges-issue-N
  -b issue-N-<slug>` and `cd` there; it never edits the lead's checkout.
- **Claiming** still happens on GitHub (assign, Status In Progress, claim
  comment, race check) *and* on the task list (claim the task). The board
  wins if they disagree.
- **Done** = the issue's acceptance list checked, hooks green, PR open with
  `Closes #N`, Status Review, summary comment; then the teammate marks the
  task completed, which unblocks dependents in the task list. The lead
  flips the board when the PR merges.
- **Team size**: 3–5 teammates at once, matching the wave widths in
  section 3. Wave 0 is one teammate; wave 1a is three; never more than
  four implementers plus one reviewer.

### 9.6 Known limits to plan around

- `/resume` does not restore in-process teammates; after a restart, the
  lead re-reads the board and the task list and respawns teammates with
  the same names and prompts.
- Task status can lag; the board is the truth, and the lead nudges.
- Teammates cannot spawn their own teams or background subagents; heavy
  parallel work inside one issue is sequential or split into issues.
- Teammate permission prompts appear in the lead's session; the
  pre-approvals in 9.1 are what keeps the team moving.
- Each teammate is a full context window; five teammates cost about five
  sessions. That is the price of parallel waves, and section 3 caps it.
